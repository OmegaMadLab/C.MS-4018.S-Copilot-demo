End-of-session Teams chat messages

At the end of the session, click Copy message for that session and paste it into the meeting chat in Teams (Ctrl+V). Bold, italics, bullets and links are kept.

If the button doesn't work in your browser, select the message text with the mouse, press Ctrl+C, then paste it into Teams.

Optional – the customer's AI policy. If the customer gave you a link to their AI or responsible-AI policy, paste it here and give it a name. Every message then gets a "Read your organisation's AI policy" line with that link. Leave it empty and the line is left out.

Session 1 – Create

📚 Copilot – Create: your take-away pack

Thank you for joining! Here are the guides and prompts from today's session.

✅ Use Copilot responsibly

  • Use only the AI tools your organisation has approved, and never put confidential data into public AI tools.
  • You stay accountable: review and verify facts, figures and sources before you share anything Copilot produced, and be transparent about where AI was used.
  • Content for external use still follows your organisation's brand, communication and approval rules.
  • Want more details? Read your organisation's AI policy: AI policy

✍️ Prompts from today

Replace the [placeholders] with your own content. Type / to reference a file.

  • Word – understand a long document (chat mode, nothing changes): Summarise this document for [audience] in [5] bullets: the key message, [our situation], the options, the recommendation and the decision needed. Mention the section each point comes from.
  • Word – ask the document a real question: Which [items] should we [prioritise], and why? Use only the data in this document and quote the figures.
  • Word – plan before you write: How would you turn this document into a [2]-page [brief] for [audience]? Propose a structure with section titles and the main content of each, and tell me what you would leave out.
  • Word – draft a brief inside your template (edit mode): Using /[source document], write a [brief/report] for [audience] in this document with this structure: [section 1], [section 2], [section 3]. Use the existing styles of this template. Maximum [2] pages. Keep every figure exactly as in the source and do not add new figures.
  • Word – turn a section into a table (select the section first): Turn this section into a table with the columns: [column 1] | [column 2] | [column 3]. Add one sentence below the table with the main takeaway.
  • Word – rewrite for a different audience (select the text first): Rewrite this [section] for [audience]: focus on [what matters to them], plain language, max [100] words.
  • Word – check before you share (chat mode): Compare this document with /[source]. Is any figure different from the source or not present in it? Which statements are assumptions rather than facts? List them with the section where they appear.
  • PowerPoint – create a deck from a document (open your company template first): Create a presentation for a [15]-minute [meeting] based on /[document]. Storyline: [slide 1], [slide 2], [slide 3]. Write each title as an action title, max [4] bullets per slide, use the layouts of this template and do not add photos of people.
  • PowerPoint – edit one slide instead of recreating the deck: Turn the content of this slide into a [chart type / table / timeline] and add one sentence of takeaway below it.
  • PowerPoint – consistency pass: Review all slide titles: make each one an action title of maximum [8] words, consistent in tone and tense. Do not change the slide content.
  • PowerPoint – talk track in the speaker notes: Write a talk track for presenting this deck in [15] minutes to [audience]: for each slide give the time to spend, the key message and 2–3 talking points, and add them to the speaker notes.
  • Copilot Chat – rehearse with a sparring partner: Act as a sceptical [role, e.g. CFO]. Based on /[file], ask me the [5] toughest questions one at a time, wait for my answer, then give me short feedback on how convincing it was and how to improve it.
  • Researcher – write a sourced report: I need to brief [audience] in a [15]-minute slot on [topic] and what we should do about it. Use reliable public sources together with my internal files /[file 1] and /[file 2] to: 1) summarise the key trends with figures; 2) compare them with our situation; 3) identify where the gap is largest; 4) propose [3] options with pros, cons, cost and timing; 5) list open questions for [teams]. Format: executive summary, tables where useful, a source for every external fact. Separate public facts, internal data and assumptions.
  • Copilot Chat – fact-check a report: Create a table of every number and date in [this report] with these columns: Claim | Source | Type (public source / internal data / assumption) | Confidence (high/medium/low). Flag anything not directly supported by a source and tell me what to verify before sharing.

💡 Tips worth stealing: chat mode to understand and check, edit mode to change – on a copy · start from your own files and templates with / · edit the slide or section, don't regenerate everything · always ask for sources and assumptions, then verify.

Session 2 – Communicate

📬 Copilot – Communicate: your take-away pack

Thank you for joining! Here are the guides and prompts from today's session.

✅ Use Copilot responsibly

  • Use only the AI tools your organisation has approved, and respect personal data and confidentiality in emails, chats and meetings.

✍️ Prompts from today

Replace the [placeholders] with your own content. Type / to reference an email, chat, meeting or file.

  • Outlook – prioritize my inbox (paste in the Prioritize settings): High priority: emails from [people]; anything about [project/topic] that asks me for [a decision/approval/input]; emails with a deadline in the next [5] working days. Low priority: [newsletters, notifications, FYIs where I'm only in CC].
  • Outlook – find what matters to me in a thread: Summarise this thread in max [6] bullets: decisions taken, open questions, and who is waiting for what from me, with deadlines.
  • Outlook – draft a reply (Draft with Copilot): Reply to [person]: [point 1]; [point 2]; [point 3]. Tone: [friendly/formal], max [120] words.
  • Copilot Chat – status across email, Teams and meetings: Give me the current status of [project] based on /[email thread], /[Teams chat] and /[meeting]. Structure: overall status (green/amber/red) with reason; progress by [workstreams]; top [3] risks; decisions needed; who owes what by when. Cite the source of every point and flag contradictions.
  • Copilot Chat – draft an update email: Draft an email to [person] with this status update, maximum [200] words, with the decisions needed in bold and a clear recommendation at the end. Put [people] in CC. Subject: "[subject]".
  • Teams – custom recap: Recap this meeting using this structure: 1) decisions (with owner) 2) actions (owner and due date, as a table) 3) what the attendees need from me 4) risks and issues 5) open questions. Max [250] words, bullet points.
  • Teams – catch up on a meeting I missed: I was invited to this meeting but could not attend. What did [people] decide about [topic], which concerns were raised, and what is still unresolved? Quote the relevant moments of the transcript.
  • Teams – find my calls to action: What do the attendees expect from me after this meeting? List every action assigned to me, with the due date and the moment in the transcript where it was said.
  • Teams – catch up on a group chat: Catch me up on this chat: what changed since [date/event], and which messages are waiting for my answer?
  • Calendar – find preparation time: Find me a [1]-hour slot before [day] to prepare [task], avoiding my existing meetings, and block it in my calendar as focus time.
  • Calendar – set up a 1:1: Set up a [45]-minute one-to-one [next week] with [person] to [purpose], at a time when we are both free. Add a short agenda: [topic 1], [topic 2], [topic 3]. Attach /[file].
  • Copilot Chat – book a meeting: Schedule a [30]-minute meeting [next week] with [people] when all are available, preferably [in the morning]. Title: "[title]". Add an agenda with [topics].
  • Copilot Chat – prepare a talk track: Write a talk track for a [15]-minute [meeting] based on [/source]: a 1-minute opening, the decisions needed with options and my recommendation, the main risks and a closing ask. Give the time for each part and [3] likely questions with short answers.
  • Scheduled prompt – Monday catch-up: Look at my inbox, calendar and Teams chats from the last [72] hours and at this week's meetings. Tell me the [3] things to start with and why, the meetings I need to prepare for and what to read first, and messages waiting for my reply for more than [2] working days. Keep it under [150] words.
  • Scheduled prompt – colour my calendar: Look at my calendar for the next 7 days and assign these categories: "[category 1]" for [rule], "[category 2]" for [rule], "[category 3]" for [rule]. Tell me what you changed.

💡 Tips worth stealing: teach Outlook what "important" means for you · ask across mail, chats and meetings in one prompt with / · Copilot drafts, you review and send · schedule your Monday catch-up.

Session 3 – Analyse

📊 Copilot – Analyse: your take-away pack

Thank you for joining! Here are the guides and prompts from today's session.

✅ Use Copilot responsibly

  • Use only the AI tools your organisation has approved, and handle personal and confidential data according to your data protection rules.
  • Outputs must be explainable: ask Copilot for the figure, formula or code behind each insight, and verify it before you share it.
  • Scores and classifications are suggestions: important decisions about people, customers or money always get a human review.

✍️ Prompts from today

Replace the [placeholders] with your own content. Type / to reference a file.

  • Excel – understand an inherited workbook (chat mode, nothing changes): Explain what this workbook contains: the purpose of each sheet, the main columns, and how the formulas in [sheet] are calculated. Point out anything that looks inconsistent or broken.
  • Excel – check the formulas: Check every formula in the [sheet] sheet. For each problem you find, tell me the cell, what is wrong, and what the correct formula should be.
  • Excel – find data-quality problems (chat mode): Check the [table] for data-quality problems: inconsistent names, impossible or out-of-range values, blank cells, negative numbers and duplicate rows. Give me a table with the issue, the rows affected and a suggested fix. Do not change anything yet.
  • Excel – clean data safely (plan mode): Clean the [table]: standardise [column] so that every value is exactly one of [value 1], [value 2], [value 3]; [fix rule]; remove exact duplicate rows; and add a column "Data check" that flags the rows that need a human decision without changing them. Show me the plan before applying it.
  • Excel – bring in data from another file: Using /[other file], create a new sheet called "[name]" with one row per [keys] and these columns: [measure] [period 1], [measure] [period 2], Change, Change %. Sort by Change, from the most negative.
  • Excel – find the drivers: Which [dimensions] drove the change in [measure] between [period 1] and [period 2]? Show the top [5] positive and negative contributors, and highlight where [cost] increased but [result] decreased.
  • Excel – one chart, one message: Create a chart that compares [measure] by [dimension] for [period 1] and [period 2], with a title that states the key message.
  • Excel – dashboard in one prompt: Create a dashboard on a new sheet called "[name]" with: [measure vs target by dimension], [comparison], [breakdown] and the top [5] [items]. Use PivotTables and charts with titles that state the key message, and fit them on one screen.
  • Excel – score text against rules: Review each [item] in the table against the criteria in the [sheet] sheet. Add columns: [criterion 1]…[criterion n] (1–5), Overall score, Main issue, Suggested improvement. Do not change the original data.
  • Analyst – precise, repeatable analysis (attach your files): Analyse [files]. Definitions and thresholds: [KPI] target [x%], critical below [y%]. 1) Show the [monthly] trend by [dimension]; 2) flag every [item] below target for [3] or more consecutive [periods] and say when it started; 3) identify the most likely drivers using [columns]; 4) summarise in max [5] bullets for [audience]; 5) create a [chart]. Show me the code you used.
  • Analyst – make the analysis repeatable: Write the exact prompt I should use next [month] so that the analysis uses the same definitions, thresholds, steps and output format and the results are comparable.
  • Excel – from insight to key messages: Write the [3] key messages for [audience] from the "[dashboard]" sheet. Each message must be one sentence with the supporting number, and suggest the best chart type to show it on a slide.

💡 Tips worth stealing: understand before you change: chat first, edit on a copy · let Copilot find data problems, keep the human decision for the ambiguous ones · reference other files instead of copying · define your thresholds: same prompt, same answer.
